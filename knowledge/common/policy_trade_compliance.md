# POL-TR-01 Import, export and cross-border movements (sample)

A purchase order from a supplier in another country is an import. Its lead time is the supplier lead time plus customs clearance in the importing country, and its landed cost includes freight and the customs duty for the item's HS code. The Incoterm on the supplier record defines where risk and cost pass to the buyer.

A transfer between sites in different countries is an export from one and an import into the other: it needs an export declaration, customs clearance time is added to transit, and import rules apply at the destination.

Items that need an import permit or conformity certificate, such as registered medicines and devices, food registrations or electronics conformity certificates, may only be imported when a valid permit for the item category and destination country is on file for the expected arrival date.

Goods may not be sourced from or shipped to a country under a trade restriction. Restrictions are checked at planning and again at execution.

When customs delays are reported, the added days are applied to every import and cross-border transfer, and slower sources are re-evaluated against the stockout risk.
